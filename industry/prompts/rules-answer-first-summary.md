
[Answer-first summary rules: make the first sentence quotable on its own by search engines and AI]

1. The first sentence must be a self-contained answer that directly says who did what and what the key result or change is; do not open with background, process, motives or the author's feelings.
   The first answer sentence must answer the core event of the original title; do not let a demo segment, a background case or a secondary feature from the body take over as the main conclusion.
   "Did what" is this item's action: when the source introduces or demos a product released earlier, the first answer sentence describes this introduction or demo, and the earlier release is only background, not written as a launch happening now.
2. Keep the first answer sentence to about 90–210 characters. It can be shorter when the source says little, but it must contain the core subject and core action; do not open with stock phrases such as "This article introduces", "The article discusses", "The author believes" or "According to reports".
3. Then add only the 1–2 most important points, ordered as verifiable facts/numbers first, then impact on users or the industry and availability. Each sentence carries one point; cut background that does not affect the conclusion.
4. Keep it to 2–3 compact declarative sentences, with no labels such as "Conclusion:" or "Key points:", and no Markdown bullets or paragraph breaks; existing cards and notifications should keep showing the summary as one paragraph.
5. When the source does not support an "impact", write only the facts and do not infer; answer-first is no excuse to add judgments the source does not contain.
