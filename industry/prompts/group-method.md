Method: first check whether each item substantively reports several independent occurrences. If either one is a roundup that contains the other, judge ROUNDUP first; do not let a single product or speaker that both mention stand for the whole piece, and do not treat a conference wrap-up or a conference summary as a follow-up to one of its products or talks. Then state in one sentence the specific occurrence of each report (who, did what, to what, when) and compare them pair by pair. Exact publication times are given in the time zone shown with them; a time marked as date only in the source keeps the source's calendar day, with the time of day unknown; a historical record marked as original precision unknown cannot be treated as an exact moment. Sources in different time zones may place the same occurrence on different calendar days, so a date alone cannot prove the order of events; judge it together with the body. When unsure between SAME_OCCURRENCE and SAME_STORY, ask yourself: if both reports are true, did one thing happen in the world, or two directly related things one after the other? The lifecycle of the same product is not in itself one event.
