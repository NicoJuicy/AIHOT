SAME_OCCURRENCE (the same occurrence): the same subject doing the same thing to the same specific object at the same time. Retellings in other languages, different outlets' emphasis, more or fewer details, and the official launch video/blog/release notes do not change the identity. Several sizes, models and base versions launched together in the same product line, plus this launch's features, prices, availability plans and system card/technical report details, count as the same occurrence; so do different reports of the same ruling, report, poll, incident, single interview or single talk, and repeated promotion of the same promotion or the same deadline. The shared identity must come down to a specific released object or a specific occurrence, not just the same company, the same day, the same conference or the same umbrella announcement: the independent product launches, different talks and different decisions within them are judged separately.

SAME_STORY (different developments of the same event): not the same occurrence, but in a direct sequence around the same specific occurrence: a teaser/rumor and the formal launch; a launch and its later listing, integration or free offer on a third-party platform; a launch and an evaluation, hands-on test, leaderboard result, first impressions or explainer about it; an event and the involved party's response, an official investigation, later disclosures or follow-up statements. Ongoing investigation or disclosure of the same exposed conduct may add affected parties, and the list of parties does not have to match in every report, but the material must support the follow-up relationship. Two independent product updates, operating results or new feature launches cannot be grouped into the same event merely because they share a product, a release cycle or come one after another.

UNRELATED (different things): two different launches of different products or different versions (different dates or different publishers); two different things by the same company on the same day; different studies, different opinions or different incidents on the same topic; merely mentioning the same product or company. A tutorial, usage tip, personal opinion or marketing post about a product is UNRELATED unless it is explicitly about the candidate's specific occurrence (evaluating it, responding to it, listing it).

ROUNDUP: one side collects several independent occurrences and treats the other side's event as one of several entries; this includes multi-topic roundups, daily and weekly digests, conference recaps, overviews of several independent products, and conference summaries of several speakers. A roundup and a single-event report it contains cannot be SAME_OCCURRENCE or SAME_STORY; mentioning two independent events together also does not mean the two events can be merged. A report that only uses other events as background or as a comparison is still judged by its core occurrence and is not automatically a roundup.
